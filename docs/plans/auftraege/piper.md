## Your topic: natural free voices in the browser (Piper TTS), one fitting voice per person
Falko (25.10.2026): „Wenn möglich, möchte ich, dass die Stimmen realistischer klingen, ohne dass sie Credits oder Geld kosten. Vielleicht findest du eine realistischere Stimme jeweils, die auch zum passenden NPC passt.“
Since today the people of the game talk freely (npc/ki.js, docs/features/ki.md: Hubert, Ronald, the eight villagers of dorf/dorf_text.js, later strollers and drivers) and every answer is spoken sentence by sentence through `audio/stimme.js` (STIMME). Its engines today: 'browser' (the device's speechSynthesis – natural only in Edge or with Apple's Premium voices, robotic elsewhere), 'eleven' (ElevenLabs, costs money), 'aus'. Your job: a third, free engine that sounds natural on every device – **Piper** (rhasspy/piper, VITS models, German voices) running in the browser with onnxruntime-web.
Goal, in this order:
1. **Feasibility first (report early, ~1 h):** pick the browser Piper library (candidates on npm: `@diffusionstudio/vits-web`, `@mintplex-labs/piper-tts-web`, `piper-tts-web`, or onnxruntime-web + a phonemizer directly – licences must allow use on a public site; note espeak-ng is GPL: check what that means for bundling and say so), measure on CI in Chromium: time to first audio for one German sentence after the model is cached, synthesis speed (real-time factor), memory, download sizes (wasm + model). German voices available from Hugging Face `rhasspy/piper-voices` (CORS-enabled, `https://huggingface.co/rhasspy/piper-voices/resolve/main/de/de_DE/<voice>/<quality>/…onnx` + `.onnx.json`): thorsten (low/medium/high), thorsten_emotional (medium, several speakers/emotions), karlsson (low), pavoque (low), kerstin (low), ramona (low), eva_k (x_low), mls (medium, multi-speaker). Listen to samples where you can (CI artifacts: write WAVs) and judge which sound natural.
2. **The engine**: a module `viz3/src/audio/piper.js` (in `viz3/src/modules.txt` directly below `audio/stimme.js`) that registers `STIMME.engine('piper', { label: 'Natürlich', note: 'kostenlos, lädt einmal die Stimmen (~… MB)', ok: () => true, buf(text, key, prof) → Promise<ArrayBuffer | AudioBuffer> (audio/stimme.js decodes encoded audio or plays an AudioBuffer as is), warm() (start loading when the player picks the engine or taps), panel() → HTML for the settings (download state, sizes, a „Stimmen laden“ button, progress), click(what, val, rerender) for your buttons })` – read `audio/stimme.js` first (how prepare/play/stop/unlock work, how profiles `{ name, g: 'm' | 'f', age }` are registered, how the settings section is drawn). The library itself goes into a package loaded on demand: `viz3/src/lazy/piper.js` (build.py bundles every `src/lazy/*.js` to `dist/lazy/*.js`, minified; load it with `import(new URL('lazy/piper.js', document.baseURI).href)` as npc/ki.js does with the SDK). The wasm runtime: self-host it in dist (copy from node_modules in build.py – minimal change – if the files are small enough for the site, say < ~15 MB total) or load it from a pinned CDN version; say which and why. Models: fetched from Hugging Face on first use, kept in the Cache Storage (no new download on the next visit), never more models in memory than needed (an iPhone has little: measure, free unused sessions). Synthesis in a Web Worker if the library allows, so the frame rate does not stutter while someone speaks.
3. **One fitting voice per person**: men and women get different voices, older people lower and slower (length_scale, and pitch via playback rate if needed), the neighbours distinct from each other (Hubert gruff, older; Ronald warm, talkative), the villagers (dorf/dorf_text.js: age, role; women: Katrin, Ingrid, Heike) as different as the models allow – with a multi-speaker model (mls, thorsten_emotional) one model can give many voices. Keep the mapping in one small data table (key → model, speaker, length_scale, pitch) with a fallback by gender and age for persons registered later; make it overridable in the settings („Stimmen der Personen“ with a probe ▶ already exists in STIMME.panel – extend it for your engine). Choose the default engine sensibly: once the models are cached and the device is fast enough, 'piper' may become the default instead of 'browser' (ask yourself: first audio within ~1 s on a laptop? on an iPhone?) – propose this in your report rather than forcing it.
- Saving only through `SAVE` (`core/save.js`) if needed (e.g. one key `pav-piper-v1`). No keys or secrets anywhere. Everything must keep working when the download fails or Hugging Face is unreachable (fall back to the browser's voice, a short note).
